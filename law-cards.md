# Law and Ethics Reference Cards

Simplified summaries for this lab. These are not legal advice. Real cases go to a lawyer.

## U.S. federal laws

| Law | Who or what it covers | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)** | Anyone | It's a crime to access a computer without authorization, or to go beyond the access you were given. |
| **Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA)** | Anyone | Restricts intercepting or accessing other people's electronic communications, such as email. |
| **HIPAA** | Only *covered entities*: health plans, health care providers and health care clearinghouses, plus their *business associates* | Protects patient health information. A breach affecting 500 or more people must be reported to HHS within 60 days. If more than 500 residents of one state are affected, the media must also be told. |
| **Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA)** | Financial institutions (banks, lenders, insurers, etc.) | Requires them to protect customers' financial information. |
| **Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX)** | Publicly traded companies | Requires accurate financial records and controls. |
| **FERPA** | Schools receiving federal funds | Protects student education records. |
| **Privacy Act of 1974** | Federal government agencies | Limits how agencies collect and share data about individuals. |
| **Economic Espionage Act (1996) / Defend Trade Secrets Act (2016)** | Anyone | Stealing trade secrets is a federal crime (EEA). Owners can also sue (DTSA). |

## Florida law

**Florida Information Protection Act of 2014 (FIPA), Fla. Stat. § 501.171**

- **Personal information** means a person's first name (or first initial) and last name together with any of:
  - a Social Security number
  - a driver's license, ID card, passport or military ID number
  - a **financial account or credit/debit card number together with any required security code (such as the CVV)**, access code or password
  - medical or health insurance information

  It also covers a username or email address together with a password or security question and answer.
- Applies to electronic data. Encrypted data generally doesn't count.
- **Notify each affected Florida resident** no later than **30 days** after the breach is discovered.
- **Notify the Florida Department of Legal Affairs (Attorney General)** within 30 days if **500 or more** Florida residents are affected.
- **Notify the nationwide consumer reporting agencies** (credit bureaus) if **more than 1,000** people must be notified at one time.
- Every other state has its own breach law for its own residents.

## International

**EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)**
- Applies to organizations anywhere in the world that offer goods or services to people in the EU.
- *Personal data* is broad: even a name and email address count.
- A breach must be reported to the EU supervisory authority within **72 hours** of becoming aware of it, unless it's unlikely to put people at risk.

## Industry standard (not a law)

**Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)**
- Created by the card brands (Visa, Mastercard and others) and enforced through **contracts** with merchants, not by the government.
- Merchants must **never store the card security code (CVV)** after a payment is approved.
- Breaking it can mean fines from the card brands and losing the ability to accept cards.

## Who to contact

| Agency | Role |
|---|---|
| **FBI** (Internet Crime Complaint Center, IC3) | Investigates federal computer crimes, including intrusions |
| **U.S. Secret Service** | Investigates financial and payment-card crimes |
| **CISA** (Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency) | Helps organizations respond to cyber incidents and protects critical infrastructure |
| **Federal Trade Commission (FTC)** | Enforces consumer protection, including companies' data security promises |
| **Florida Department of Legal Affairs** | Receives Florida breach notices (500+ residents) |

## Ethics

**(ISC)² Code of Ethics, the four canons (in priority order)**
1. Protect society, the common good, necessary public trust and confidence, and the infrastructure.
2. Act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly and legally.
3. Provide diligent and competent service to principals.
4. Advance and protect the profession.

**Deterrence** (from Module 6). Laws and policies deter people best when all three conditions exist:
1. Fear of the penalty
2. A real chance of being caught
3. A real chance the penalty will be applied
